Huge phone photos slow down websites. Before you compress or convert formats, resize to the width you actually need. You can do that in the browser without uploading the file.

## Why resize before compress

- Fewer pixels usually means a much smaller file
- Compression alone cannot fix a 4000px image used at 800px
- Consistent widths keep blog layouts tidy

## Common web widths

| Use | Typical max width |
|--|--|
| Blog content image | 1200–1600px |
| Hero / banner | 1600–2400px (depends on design) |
| Thumbnail / card | 400–800px |
| Avatar | 128–512px |

Keep **aspect ratio locked** unless you intentionally want to stretch the image.

## Steps (local)

1. Open [Image Resize](/tools/image-resize).
2. Choose your JPG, PNG, or WebP.
3. Enter the target width (height can follow if aspect ratio is locked).
4. Download the resized file.
5. Optional next steps:
   - [Compress Image Online](/tools/compress-image-online)
   - [JPG to WebP](/tools/jpg-to-webp) or [PNG to WebP](/tools/png-to-webp)
   - [JPG to PNG](/tools/jpg-to-png) if your editor prefers PNG

Everything runs on your device.

## Tips

- Resize down for the web; upsizing a small image will look soft.
- For screenshots with text, avoid aggressive compression after resize.
- Batch convert formats after you have the right dimensions.
