PNG is great for screenshots, UI mockups, and graphics with sharp text. The downside is file size — a single screenshot can be several megabytes, which is awkward for email, tickets, or upload forms.

You do not always need a desktop editor. A browser tool can compress the file on your device so nothing is uploaded to a random server.

## Why PNGs get large

- Lots of colors and soft gradients increase size
- Full-resolution Retina screenshots store more pixels than you need
- Transparency adds data compared with a flat JPG photo

## A simple workflow

1. **Resize first if the image is bigger than you need.**  
   A 4000px-wide screenshot rarely needs to stay that wide for a blog or form. Use [Image Resize](/tools/image-resize) and keep aspect ratio locked.
2. **Compress next.**  
   Open [Image Compress](/tools/image-compress), choose your PNG, and start around 70% quality. Download and check the result.
3. **Compare visually.**  
   Zoom in on text and icons. If edges look muddy, raise quality slightly. If the file is still huge, lower quality or resize again.

## PNG vs JPG for compression

- Keep **PNG** when you need sharp text, icons, or transparency.
- Switch to **JPG** when the image is mostly a photo and slight softness is acceptable — JPG often wins on size.
