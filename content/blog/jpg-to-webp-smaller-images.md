JPG is everywhere — phone photos, blog uploads, product shots. WebP is a modern format that often delivers a similar look at a smaller file size. If your site feels slow because of heavy images, JPG → WebP is one of the easiest wins.

## Why switch JPG to WebP

- Faster page loads on blogs and landing pages
- Lower bandwidth on mobile networks
- Better Core Web Vitals when images are a bottleneck
- Still sharp enough for most photo content

Modern browsers support WebP well. If you need universal compatibility for email or old software, keep a JPG copy or convert back later.

## Local conversion (no upload)

1. Open the [JPG to WebP converter](/tools/jpg-to-webp).
2. Optionally set quality (start around 80–90%).
3. Select one JPG — or many for batch convert.
4. Download each WebP, or download a ZIP of all results.

QuickFile Tools runs in your browser. Your photos are not uploaded to our servers.

## Quality tips

- For hero photos, try **85–92%** and compare visually.
- For thumbnails, you can go lower to save more space.
- If the WebP looks soft, raise quality slightly instead of staying at a very low setting.
- Still too large? Resize first with [Image Resize](/tools/image-resize), then convert.

## JPG vs WebP

| | JPG | WebP |
|--|--|--|
| Typical size | Larger at similar quality | Often smaller |
| Best for | Universal sharing | Web delivery |
| Transparency | No | Yes (when applicable) |
| On QuickFile | Local convert | Local convert |

## When to use PNG instead

Keep or export **PNG** for logos, screenshots with text, or graphics that need sharp edges and transparency. For photos destined for a website, WebP (or compressed JPG) is usually the better fit.

Need the reverse direction? Use [WebP to PNG](/tools/webp-to-png) when an editor or workflow requires PNG.
